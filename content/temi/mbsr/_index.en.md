---
title: "MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction)"
---

Think of MBSR as a swimming course designed specifically for people who have to face rough water — stress, pain, illness: it does not teach everything one could know about swimming, or about Buddhist meditation as a whole; it teaches the techniques useful for staying afloat in that specific situation.

## Essential Definition

MBSR, Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, is a structured eight-week program, developed in a medical setting in 1979, that teaches meditation techniques derived from Buddhism to help people manage stress, chronic pain, and illness.

## In Context

MBSR was founded by Jon Kabat-Zinn in 1979 at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center. The program strips Buddhist meditation of every religious reference and translates it into clinical language. Kabat-Zinn himself does not describe this operation as an accidental loss of Buddhist elements, but as a conscious application of the concept of *upāya* (see dedicated entry): adapting a teaching to the context and abilities of those who receive it, while remaining faithful to its substance (Kabat-Zinn, 2011).

Many related programs derive from MBSR: MBCT for preventing depressive relapse, MBRP for addiction, and the many corporate "workplace mindfulness" programs. Here, general-audience treatments often make a mistake: they treat "corporate mindfulness" as if it were simply MBSR brought into the office. It is not. Corporate programs are usually shorter, less structured versions, without the clinical supervision of the original program — a difference that popular accounts tend not to flag.

If MBSR is the full swimming course, eight weeks with a qualified instructor, many corporate mindfulness programs resemble more a twenty-minute tutorial video on the same subject. They come from the same source, but they are not the same thing, and they do not offer the same guarantees.
