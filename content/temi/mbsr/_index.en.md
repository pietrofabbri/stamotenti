---
title: "MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction)"
---

MBSR is like a swimming course designed specifically for people who have to face rough waters (stress, pain, illness): it doesn’t teach everything there is to know about swimming, or about Buddhist meditation in its entirety, but rather teaches the techniques needed to stay afloat in that specific situation.

## Essential Definition

**MBSR, *Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction*, is a structured eight-week program, developed in the medical field in 1979, that teaches meditation techniques derived from Buddhism to help people manage stress, chronic pain, and illness.**

## History of the Term

“MBSR” is an acronym, and the program’s name consists of two parts: *stress reduction*, which indicates the clinical purpose, and *mindfulness-based* (see the entry on *Mindfulness*), which indicates the method. The name, coined with the program in 1979 (Kabat-Zinn 2011), contains no reference to the Buddhism from which the practice derives, in line with the translation into clinical language described below.

## In Context

MBSR was founded by Jon Kabat-Zinn in 1979 at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center. The program removes all religious references from Buddhist meditation and translates it into clinical language. Kabat-Zinn himself does not describe this process as an accidental loss of Buddhist elements, but as a conscious application of the concept of *upāya* (see dedicated entry): adapting a teaching to the context and capabilities of those receiving it, while remaining faithful to its essence (Kabat-Zinn, 2011).

Many related programs have emerged from MBSR: MBCT for the prevention of depressive relapse, MBRP for addictions, and numerous corporate “mindfulness at work” programs. This is where generalist approaches often go wrong: they treat “corporate mindfulness” as if it were simply MBSR brought into the office. It is not. Corporate programs are usually shorter, less structured versions that lack the clinical supervision of the original program: a difference that popular reports tend not to mention.

If MBSR is the full swimming course (eight weeks with a qualified instructor), many corporate mindfulness programs are more like a twenty-minute video tutorial on the same topic. They stem from the same source, but they are not the same thing, and they do not offer the same guarantees.
