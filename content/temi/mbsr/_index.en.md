title: "MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction)"
---

Think of MBSR as a swimming class designed specifically for those who have to face rough waters (stress, pain, illness): it doesn’t teach everything there is to know about swimming—or about Buddhist meditation in its entirety—but rather teaches the techniques needed to stay afloat in that specific situation.

## Essential Definition

**MBSR, *Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction*, is an eight-week structured program, developed in a medical setting in 1979, that teaches meditation techniques derived from Buddhism to help people manage stress, chronic pain, and illness.**

## Context of the Entry

MBSR was founded by Jon Kabat-Zinn in 1979 at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center. The program strips Buddhist meditation of all religious references and translates it into clinical language. **Kabat-Zinn himself does not describe this process as an accidental loss of Buddhist elements, but as a conscious application of the concept of *upāya* (see dedicated entry): adapting a teaching to the context and capabilities of the recipient, while remaining faithful to its essence** (Kabat-Zinn, 2011).

Many related programs have emerged from MBSR: MBCT for the prevention of depressive relapse, MBRP for addictions, and numerous corporate “mindfulness at work” programs. This is where generalist approaches often go wrong: they treat “corporate mindfulness” as if it were simply MBSR brought into the office. It is not. **Corporate programs are usually shorter, less structured versions that lack the clinical supervision of the original program—a difference that popular accounts tend not to mention.**

If MBSR is the full-fledged swimming course—eight weeks with a qualified instructor—many corporate mindfulness programs are more like a twenty-minute video tutorial on the same topic. **They stem from the same source, but they are not the same thing, and they do not offer the same guarantees.**
